## 2. Company / money flow
CIT was a commercial-finance company serving middle-market and asset-heavy borrowers. Its economics depend on borrowing costs, asset yields, credit losses, leverage, securitization/wholesale funding access and the liquidity value of its loan/lease book. Unlike a deposit-rich bank, a finance company can look profitable until wholesale funding suddenly reprices or disappears.

## 3. 2001 context
The post-dot-com slowdown increased corporate-credit risk and exposed funding-sensitive lenders. A valid bearish thesis would need to connect borrower deterioration to both credit costs and CIT's own liability structure. A mere recession call is not enough because finance companies can offset some credit pressure through pricing, asset sales or funding diversification.

## 4. Thesis map
### C1 — credit deterioration
**Claim:** middle-market/cyclical borrowers may default more than reserves imply.
**Falsifier:** delinquencies and charge-offs stabilize while pricing compensates for risk.

### C2 — funding fragility
**Claim:** wholesale funding can become more expensive or less available exactly when asset quality weakens.
**Falsifier:** term funding remains ample and maturities are extended at acceptable spreads.

### C3 — leverage amplifies small asset mistakes
A few percentage points of asset impairment can create a much larger change in common-equity value.

### C4 — asset liquidity matters
Loans and leases may have economic value but cannot necessarily be monetized at carrying value during stress.

## 5. Capital-structure model
A CIT analysis should always map:
- secured vs unsecured funding;
- maturity ladder;
- commercial paper and bank facilities;
- securitization capacity;
- unrestricted cash;
- asset encumbrance;
- rating triggers;
- covenant/headroom;
- expected recoveries by security class.

## 6. Actual-history lens
CIT ultimately experienced a severe funding crisis during the 2008–09 financial crisis and filed for bankruptcy protection in 2009 before reorganizing. That later event demonstrates the importance of liability-side analysis, but it must not be used to retroactively validate a 2001 trade without checking the original horizon and security.

## 7. First falsification signals
Falling funding spreads, successful term issuance, improving charge-offs, stronger liquidity, asset sales near book, and ratings stabilization.
