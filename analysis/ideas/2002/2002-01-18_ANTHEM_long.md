## 2. Company / money flow
Anthem was a US managed-care insurer. The economics are premium revenue less medical claims, selling/admin expenses and required capital. The key operating variable is the spread between premium pricing and medical cost trend, expressed through the medical cost ratio. Scale matters because provider contracting, administrative cost absorption, data and product breadth can improve unit economics, but scale does not eliminate underwriting-cycle risk.

## 3. 2002 market context
The early-2000s managed-care setup combined pricing recovery after prior underpricing with consolidation. A value thesis in this period must therefore distinguish cyclical margin repair from durable structural improvement. The correct question is not simply whether EPS grows, but whether premium repricing exceeds medical inflation long enough to create sustainable ROE and strategic value.

## 4. Thesis map
### C1 — underwriting/pricing recovery
**Claim:** premium pricing should catch up with medical cost trend.
**Hidden assumption:** competitors remain disciplined and employers accept increases.
**Falsifier:** medical cost ratio rises despite repricing.

### C2 — scale creates strategic value
**Claim:** larger membership and regional density should improve negotiating leverage and fixed-cost absorption.
**Hidden assumption:** integration and provider relationships do not destroy the scale benefit.
**Falsifier:** SG&A and medical cost ratios fail to improve as scale rises.

### C3 — consolidation can crystallize value
**Claim:** managed-care consolidation can create a strategic exit multiple above standalone value.
**Hidden assumption:** regulatory approval and integration risk remain manageable.
**Actual terminal event:** Anthem combined with WellPoint Health Networks in 2004 and the combined company adopted the WellPoint name.

## 5. Valuation / payoff
The right framework is normalized EPS/ROE after medical-cost normalization plus strategic value from scale. A low headline P/E during a favorable pricing cycle can be misleading if the medical-cost ratio is temporarily depressed; conversely, a reasonable multiple can still be attractive if pricing discipline and consolidation materially improve long-run economics.

## 7. Postmortem
**Business thesis:** likely correct — scale and managed-care economics remained valuable.
**Valuation thesis:** directionally favorable, but exact T0 valuation still needs original body reconstruction.
**Catalyst/timing:** consolidation became a real catalyst within roughly three years.
**Security selection:** common equity matched the operating thesis.

## 8. First falsification signals to monitor ex ante
Medical-cost ratio, premium yield vs medical trend, membership losses, SG&A leverage, state/federal regulatory resistance to consolidation.

## 9. Reusable lesson
For health insurers, apparent earnings growth should be decomposed into pricing, claims trend, membership and capital intensity. The best long theses are usually strongest when both underwriting normalization and strategic scale economics point in the same direction.
