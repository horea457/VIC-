## 2. Business model
Athene sources retirement liabilities through retail annuities, institutional channels and reinsurance. It invests the resulting float across fixed income and alternative/structured assets and earns the residual spread. The core equation is not premium growth but **asset yield – liability crediting/hedging – operating cost – realized credit cost – capital cost**.

## 3. Thesis map
### C1 — normalized spread
A thesis must distinguish contractual/liability cost from temporary accounting spread. If new-money yields fall or liability costs are sticky, earnings power compresses.

### C2 — asset risk
Higher portfolio yield can reflect sourcing skill or embedded illiquidity/credit risk. The correct test is realized loss, migration, marks and capital consumption through a cycle.

### C3 — capital allocation
If shares trade below conservatively adjusted book, repurchases can be highly accretive. A bearish valuation thesis therefore needs to explain why management cannot or should not arbitrage the discount.

### C4 — Apollo relationship
Apollo provides origination and asset-management capabilities, but fees and governance should be incorporated explicitly in normalized ROE.

## 4. 2019 context
Rates were declining and investors were skeptical of life/annuity balance sheets. This created an apparent paradox: lower rates pressured reinvestment economics, while low valuation and strong capital could make repurchases attractive. The investment case therefore depended on which effect dominated.

## 5. Hidden assumptions
- credit losses remain close to modeled levels;
- annuity liabilities remain sufficiently sticky;
- statutory/rating capital does not bind;
- adjusted book value is a reasonable proxy for economic capital;
- Apollo-related economics remain acceptable to minority holders.

## 6. Actual timeline
2019 was followed by the severe 2020 market shock, providing a direct stress test of the asset/liability model. Athene survived that shock and in 2021 agreed to combine with Apollo in an all-stock transaction completed in 2022. The standalone ATH security therefore had a finite terminal event.

## 7. Postmortem questions
1. Did credit losses exceed the bearish model or remain manageable?
2. Did spread compression occur because of rates, or was it offset by asset sourcing?
3. Was the discount to book justified by hidden risk, or did buybacks/book-value growth make it self-correcting?
4. Did the Apollo combination transfer value fairly to ATH holders?

## 9. Reusable lesson
For annuity insurers, a low P/B thesis is incomplete without a **capital-duration-credit bridge**. The same spread widening that hurts marks can improve future reinvestment returns if capital survives.
