## 2. Company / money flow
Athabasca was a Canadian oil-sands/development company. The economics are unlike a mature producer: value sits in undeveloped reserves/resources, project sanction probability, capex requirements, infrastructure access and future commodity prices. Equity behaves like a long-dated option on resource monetization, but that option is funded with real cash burn and can be diluted if project timelines slip.

## 3. 2011 market context
Oil sands were still valued on large resource bases and long-duration development optionality. The danger in a short thesis was that strategic buyers could pay for acreage or future barrels well before current cash flow justified the valuation. The danger in a long thesis was the opposite: headline NAV could embed optimistic oil prices, timing and discount rates while ignoring financing and execution risk.

## 4. Thesis map
### C1 — resource NAV may overstate equity value
A project-level NAV is only relevant after deducting all remaining capex, taxes, royalties, infrastructure and financing costs.

### C2 — time is a cost
Each year of delay reduces present value and raises the probability of dilution or asset sales. A common analytical error is to discount project cash flows but not explicitly model the financing path required to reach first production.

### C3 — oil-price sensitivity is nonlinear
High fixed development costs mean a moderate decline in long-term oil assumptions can destroy a disproportionate amount of equity value.

### C4 — strategic optionality can break a short
Partner transactions, farm-outs or asset sales can validate acreage value even when standalone FCF is weak.

## 5. Payoff tree
**Bear case:** project delays + weaker oil + rising capex → NAV compression and funding stress.
**Base case:** gradual development with asset monetization → equity retains option value but rerating is limited.
**Bull case:** strategic partner/asset sale at attractive implied resource value → short thesis fails quickly.

## 7. Actual-history framework
Athabasca survived and continued as a Canadian upstream company; the 2014–16 oil-price collapse provided a severe stress test for high-duration oil-sands optionality. The right postmortem must separate whether the 2011 thesis was structurally correct from whether a 12–24 month short actually earned an attractive return before later commodity-cycle events.

## 8. First falsification signals
Partner transactions above bearish NAV, lower-than-expected capex, faster sanctioning, strong balance-sheet funding, sustained high oil prices.

## 9. Reusable lesson
For pre-FCF resource companies, **NAV is not equity value until the funding bridge is modeled**. The most important variables are often time-to-cash-flow and financing, not the size of the resource base.
