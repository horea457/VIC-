## 2. Company / value chain
Athabasca owns/owned Canadian oil-sands and conventional assets. A development-stage oil company converts acreage and reserves into equity value only after permitting, project sanction, capex funding, construction, ramp-up and commodity realization. The gap between geological value and realizable shareholder value can be enormous.

## 3. 2013 setup
By late 2013, oil prices were still high enough to support large resource NAVs, but the market had become more selective about capital intensity and execution. A short thesis in this setting likely depended less on proving the resource worthless and more on proving that the equity was capitalizing too much future value too early.

## 4. Thesis map
### C1 — headline resource value vs funded NAV
If the market values barrels before financing and build-out, equity can trade above economic NAV.

### C2 — partner/asset-sale dependence
A credible strategic transaction can validate value; failure or delay can expose how much of the valuation rested on monetization rather than operating cash flow.

### C3 — commodity-price duration
Projects with multi-year lead times are effectively long-duration assets. Discount rates and long-term oil assumptions matter more than near-term spot prices.

### C4 — capital intensity creates equity convexity both ways
Lower capex or better funding can sharply raise equity value. Conversely, capex inflation or delay can erase common-equity value despite unchanged resources.

## 5. Actual-history lens
The subsequent 2014 oil-price collapse was exactly the type of macro shock that stresses high-duration resource equities. But a proper postmortem must avoid hindsight: the 2013 idea should be judged on information available at T0, and on whether the bearish thesis had identifiable company-specific falsifiers before the macro collapse.

## 6. Valuation method
Use project-by-project risked NAV with explicit assumptions for remaining capex, first production date, decline/ramp, royalties, operating cost, transportation differentials, WTI/WCS price deck and discount rate. Then subtract corporate costs and net debt and model funding/dilution separately.

## 7. Falsifiers
Strategic sale at a premium to bearish NAV; materially lower capex; acceleration of regulatory approvals; stronger liquidity; sustained high realized pricing.
