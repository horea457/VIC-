## 2. Why August 2007 matters
This post sits at the opening phase of the global credit crisis. For a wholesale-funded finance company, the analytical priority changes abruptly from normalized ROE to survival mechanics: funding access, collateral, asset sales, rating triggers and maturity walls become more important than reported earnings.

## 3. Company / money flow
CIT finances middle-market companies, equipment and other commercial assets. It earns a spread between asset yields and funding costs, but must continuously refinance a meaningful liability stack. The model is therefore exposed to a dangerous feedback loop:

**credit concerns → funding spreads widen → earnings weaken → ratings pressure rises → collateral/funding access deteriorates → forced deleveraging → book-value loss.**

## 4. Thesis map
### C1 — funding cost repricing
If debt markets reprice faster than CIT can reprice assets, net interest margin compresses.

### C2 — liquidity duration mismatch
Longer-duration loans funded with shorter wholesale liabilities create refinancing risk even before credit losses are realized.

### C3 — asset marks vs realizable value
Assets that appear money-good at maturity may still have to be sold below book if liquidity is needed today.

### C4 — rating-agency reflexivity
A downgrade can itself worsen funding economics, creating a self-reinforcing cycle.

### C5 — security-specific payoff
A bearish enterprise thesis does not imply every CIT security is a good short. Senior secured, unsecured, preferred and common can have radically different recoveries and carry.

## 5. Hidden assumptions a short must make
- funding markets remain stressed long enough to matter;
- secured liquidity is insufficient to bridge maturities;
- asset sales occur below carrying value;
- government or strategic support does not fully neutralize the liquidity gap;
- borrow/carry on the chosen security is manageable.

## 6. Actual-history lens
CIT's problems ultimately deepened dramatically during 2008–09, ending in a 2009 bankruptcy reorganization. That makes the liability-side thesis historically important. But a rigorous postmortem still needs to measure whether the 2007 idea earned an attractive return within its intended horizon and whether the chosen security captured the enterprise deterioration efficiently.

## 7. First falsification signals
Successful unsecured issuance, stable ratings, large committed liquidity, asset sales near book, falling CDS/funding spreads, deposit or bank-funding diversification.

## 8. Valuation / payoff framework
For stressed lenders, replace conventional P/E with a waterfall:
1. stressed asset value;
2. encumbered vs unencumbered collateral;
3. liquidity through maturity dates;
4. secured claims;
5. senior unsecured claims;
6. preferred/sub debt;
7. residual common equity.

When a lender's liabilities can run faster than its assets mature, **enterprise solvency is not enough**. Liquidity, collateral and refinancing sequence determine equity and debt outcomes.
